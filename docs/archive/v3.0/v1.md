## What this proves and what it doesn't

**Proves:**

- The full pipeline works end-to-end: ingestion → cleaning → MediaPipe
  keypoint extraction → classifier training → ONNX export → browser
  inference.
- The classifier learns something meaningful — top-3 at 36% is **~10×
  random** for an 80-class problem.
- The eval-gate enforcer (`scripts/check_eval_gate.py`) correctly
  identifies failure and refuses to promote.

**Does not prove:**

- That the recognition model meets production-grade accuracy at
  slice-1 scope.

## Root cause analysis

The slice-1 data pipeline produced **1,107 cleaned clips across 80
signs** — an average of ~11 clips/sign before splitting, ~10 in the
training set after the 70/15/15 stratification. ADR 0006 set the
target at 50–80 clips/sign; we are at roughly 1/5 of that, with
public sources only (ADR 0008).

| Source | Clips |
|---|---|
| WLASL (Li et al. 2020) | 668 |
| Lifeprint embeds (Bill Vicars, sourced via Lifeprint per ADR 0004) | 145 |
| YouTube tutorial search ("how to sign X in ASL") | 294 |

Two factors dominate the accuracy ceiling:

1. **Insufficient training data per sign.** The from-scratch BiLSTM
   has ~700K parameters; ~10 training clips per sign is roughly
   1/5 of what landmark-based classifiers at this vocabulary scale
   typically need.
2. **MediaPipe detection failure on ~40% of frames.** This is the
   single largest failure mode. Several causes:
   - WLASL clips are sourced from research-dataset YouTube videos
     with variable framing; many frames show signers' hands at rest
     between signs or partially out of frame.
   - The cleaning pipeline samples 16 frames evenly across each
     clip — when a sign's actual motion lasts only ~1 second of a
     2-second clip, half the sampled frames are pre/post-sign
     "rest" frames where MediaPipe legitimately sees no hands.
   - Center-square cropping during normalization sometimes removes
     peripheral hand regions on signs that travel.

We disclose this rather than paper it over (per ADR 0006's "honest
disclosure about landmark-based fairness" commitment + ADR 0008).

## What slice-2 work would change

The slice-1 limitations are direct consequences of ADR-0004 (no
instructor) and ADR-0008 (no self-recording). The slice-2 production-
deployment work named in those ADRs addresses both:

1. **Deaf instructor recordings** with the green-box framing the
   classifier was designed against. Expected effect on MediaPipe
   detection rate: 40% miss → ~5% (the eval gate's criterion 10).
   Expected effect on top-1: at 50–80 clips/sign with clean
   detection, 80–90% top-1 is realistic per ADR 0006.
2. **In-app community feedback channel** flagging false-negative
   signs, driving targeted re-collection.
3. **Parameter-aware hint head** (ADR 0006) — second classifier
   head over the same keypoint sequences, trained with no
   additional data, predicting handshape/location/orientation/
   movement separately. Allows hints to name *which parameter was
   wrong*, not just *which sign was predicted*.
